ROUTER TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE

DEVICE: Router
CATEGORY: Network Connectivity

SYMPTOM: Router keeps disconnecting

COMMON CAUSES:
- Router overheating
- Loose or damaged network cable
- ISP connection instability
- Outdated router firmware
- Weak Wi-Fi signal
- Too many connected devices
- Router configuration problems

DIAGNOSTIC STEPS:

STEP 1:
Restart the router.
Turn off the router, wait approximately 30 seconds, and turn it back on.
Wait until the Internet/WAN indicator becomes stable.

STEP 2:
Check the cables.
Make sure the power cable and Internet/WAN cable are firmly connected.

STEP 3:
Check whether the problem affects all devices.
If only one device disconnects, the problem may be related to that device rather than the router.

STEP 4:
Check router temperature.
Make sure the router is placed in a ventilated location and is not overheating.

STEP 5:
Reduce connected devices.
Temporarily disconnect unnecessary devices and test the connection again.

STEP 6:
Check Internet/WAN status.
If the router shows an Internet/WAN error, the problem may be related to the Internet Service Provider.

STEP 7:
Check firmware.
If the problem continues, check whether a firmware update is available for the router.

ESCALATION:
Escalate to human technical support when:
- The router repeatedly disconnects after troubleshooting.
- The WAN/Internet connection remains unavailable.
- The router shows hardware failure.
- The customer cannot access router configuration.
- The customer reports a safety or electrical issue.
